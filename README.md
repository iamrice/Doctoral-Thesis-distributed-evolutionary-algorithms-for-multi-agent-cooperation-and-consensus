## Abstract

With the rapid development of artificial intelligence and complex networked systems, swarm
intelligence and autonomous cooperative systems have gradually become important directions in
international technological competition. Multi-agent systems enable multiple agents with sensing,
computing, and execution capabilities to collaboratively accomplish complex tasks, showing broad
application prospects in unmanned system swarms, smart grids, wireless sensor networks, and other
domains. Although significant progress has been made in distributed optimization and multi-agent
cooperative control in recent years, several key challenges remain when addressing real-world
complex systems: (1) global consensus in black-box decentralized scenarios is difficult; (2) coordinated
regulation of sampling exploration step sizes is difficult; (3) cooperative optimization under conflicting
local interests is difficult.

In response to the above challenges, this dissertation is grounded in evolutionary computation and
multi-agent consensus theory, and focuses on black-box distributed optimization problems. Following
the research paradigm of "population evolutionary collaboration – sampling search collaboration –
objective incentive collaboration", a collaborative evolutionary optimization framework suitable for
decentralized multi-agent systems is constructed. The main contributions are: (i) a multi-agent
consensus optimization algorithm based on an internal–external collaborative learning mechanism;
(ii) a multi-agent consensus optimization algorithm with a cooperative and cumulative step-size
adaptation mechanism; (iii) a multi-agent co-evolutionary algorithm with a penalty-based objective
for conflicting local objectives; (iv) a contribution-based cooperation scheme for distributed
multi-target localization and data association in wireless sensor networks; and (v) a multi-agent
swarm optimization method for decentralized energy management in smart grids considering game
behaviors of electric vehicles.

**Keywords:** multi-agent systems; decentralized systems; distributed optimization; evolutionary
computation; consensus theory; wireless sensor networks; smart grids
